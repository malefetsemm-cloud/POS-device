Overview

I worked on an inventory management solution for a POS device, designed to help merchants manage stock, process transactions, and gain better visibility into their store operations.

The project focused on creating a more seamless experience for merchants while also improving the customer checkout journey. A key part of the challenge was balancing established design-system standards with business requirements that were not fully supported by the existing design library.

Through research, collaboration, iteration, and close partnership with development, we delivered a solution that met the needs of the business while maintaining a coherent user experience. The product went live and has proven successful in-market.

A key challenge: design system vs. business requirements

"What the design system says we should do"and"What the product needs to do to solve the business problem."

The tension

  • The design library gave us consistency and established standards, but it couldn't accommodate every business requirement.
  • Some workflows the business needed didn't fit neatly into existing patterns.
  • Rather than treat the system as unchallengeable, we looked at where it helped and where it blocked the real user and business problem.

The approach

  • Matched requirements that existing components could solve.
  • Isolated the requirements the library couldn't adequately support.
  • Explored alternative interaction patterns for those gaps.
  • Deliberated with the wider design team on where standards could reasonably be challenged.
  • Worked with development to confirm technical feasibility.
  • Iterated until consistency, usability and business needs were balanced.

In some cases we broke standards deliberately, as conscious exceptions where the system couldn't support the requirement, not for design preference.
